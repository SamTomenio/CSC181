### Selected problem

Electricity consumers, student boarders, and residential households in Iligan City experience severe financial uncertainty and bill shocks due to the absence of accessible reverse-budgeting tools that convert fixed cash limits into daily appliance operating hours.

### Selection rationale

We selected the first candidate problem because it directly addresses an immediate, high-friction financial risk that regularly impacts a broad base of Iligan City electricity consumers. With direct access to students, boarders, landlords, and local families, our team can easily gather empirical data on household energy budgeting habits, whereas hazard reporting and informational mapping rely heavily on municipal department cooperation and city-maintained datasets that are difficult to access within a single semester. Furthermore, analyzing and addressing this problem falls well within our team's analytical and technical readiness, allowing us to evaluate consumer pain points independently without requiring unreleased utility APIs. Ultimately, the other candidate problems were passed over due to high administrative dependencies, whereas electricity bill shocks represent a pressing, self-contained problem that we can effectively research and solve within the project timeframe.

## 3. Preliminary Project Proposal

### Working project title

**Iligan WattsApp: Personal Energy Reverse-Budgeting Calculator for ILPI Consumers**

### Problem statement

Residential electricity consumers, budget-conscious families, and boarding house tenants in Iligan City face financial uncertainty because they have difficulty translating a fixed electricity budget into practical appliance-use limits. Users often rely on rough estimates, forward electricity calculators, landlord notices, or physical meter checks, which do not directly answer how many hours a selected appliance can be used within a remaining Peso allowance. This makes it difficult to plan household activities and anticipate end-of-month electricity expenses. The proposed improvement is an offline-capable mobile application that lets users configure electricity rates and appliance wattages, estimate planned daily and monthly costs, and calculate affordable operating hours using reverse-budgeting formulas. Its estimates depend on user-provided information rather than live meter readings. The team must still validate whether users can enter reliable appliance specifications and understand the limitations of these estimates.

### Proposed solution

A mobile application designed for Iligan City electricity consumers that operates without requiring expensive smart hardware or direct utility API access. The app uses **client-side calculation algorithms** and **local device storage (SQLite/Hive)** to solve appliance budget planning. The app supports **electricity budget planning** by converting a target Peso limit ($PHP$) into estimated allowable daily operating hours for selected appliances using reverse math. Users can adjust usage parameters to simulate trade-offs between household appliances to maintain their target electricity budget.

## 5. Initial Scope

### Intended users and setting

The intended users are residential electricity consumers in the City of Iligan. In particular it would be budget conscious households, families, and boarding house tenants, especially students who want to manage their electricity consumption and avoid end-of-month bill shocks. Based on the initial interviews and surveys, many consumers rely on physical bills, landlord notices, or rough estimates to monitor their electricity consumption, making it difficult to anticipate expenses before the billing period ends. The application is intended to operate on personal mobile devices, providing users with an accessible way to estimate appliance electricity consumption, project daily and monthly expenses, and manage their budgets without requiring direct access to ILPI systems, physical electricity meters, or an internet connection.

### In-scope capabilities

1. **Electricity Reverse Budget Calculator:** Users enter a monthly electricity budget, their estimated electricity rate, and appliance wattages to calculate approximate affordable daily operating hours for a selected appliance after accounting for other planned use.
2. **Appliance Planner and Cost Simulator:** Users add appliances, set wattages and daily usage, and see estimated daily consumption and projected monthly electricity costs against their budget.
3. **Flexible Budget and Usage Management:** Users set monthly and weekly planning targets and identify essential or flexible appliances when considering usage adjustments.
4. **Locally Saved Tracker:** Users save electricity budgets, appliance information, and plans on their device for later review without requiring an online account.
5. **Preset Appliance Library:** Users select offline appliance wattage examples and adjust them to match their devices.

### Out-of-scope capabilities

1. **Electricity Consumption Prediction Using Machine Learning:** The application will not implement machine learning models to predict future household electricity consumption based on historical behavior. Its calculations will use predefined electrical formulas and user-configured consumption parameters  
2. **Cloud Synchronization and Multi-Device Account Management :** The application will not provide cloud-based synchronization of appliance records, budgets, and consumption histories across multiple devices. User information will primarily be stored locally on the device.  
3. **Automatic Real Time Electricity Consumption Monitoring**: The application will not directly measure the actual electricity consumption of household appliances or automatically retrieve real-time meter readings through external hardware. Consumption figures and bill projections will be calculated using user-provided appliance information, estimated operating hours, and configured electricity rates.  
4. **Remote Appliance Control and Automation:** The application will not remotely operate, schedule, or automatically switch household appliances on or off. It will only provide calculations and recommendations to help users plan their appliance usage. This is to mean that this is fully digital and will not in anyway have physical effects on interactions.

### Expected deliverables

1. A working mobile application prototype with the aforementioned expected functionality. Noticeably in their prototype form.  
2. A local database for saving appliance records, budget plans, and calculations.  
3. User interface designs and documentation describing the application's features, calculations, limitations, and intended workflows.  
4. A technical document explaining the reverse budgeting formulas and data storage structure.  
5. Testing documentation covering calculation accuracy, data persistence, and usability.  
6. A final project report presenting the implementation, testing results, user feedback, limitations, and recommendations for future development.

### Constraints


| Constraint | Effect on the project | How the team will respond |
|---| :---- |---|
| **Dependence on user-entered information** | Incorrect or incomplete user entries can reduce the reliability of consumption estimates and calculated operating hours. | Provide input validation, clear instructions, and warnings that calculations are estimates based on user-entered data rather than official utility readings. |
| **Variations in electricity rates and billing arrangements**  | Residential consumers and boarding house tenants may have different effective electricity rates due to landlord-set rates, sub-meter arrangements, and changes in electricity costs. Using an inaccurate rate may result in misleading cost projections.  | Allow users to manually configure and update their effective electricity rates. Clearly identify the rate used in calculations and provide an option to compare estimates with actual bills.  |
| **Variations in actual appliance consumption**  | Appliances such as inverter air conditioners and refrigerators may not consistently operate at their rated wattage, making calculations based solely on wattage and operating hours less precise.  | Clearly communicate estimation limitations, allow users to adjust appliance information and usage assumptions, and avoid presenting calculated consumption as actual measured electricity usage.  |

## 6. Review and Next Steps

### Change made after reviewing the proposal

* Before review: "App predicts total end-of-month utility bills."  
* After review: "App calculates allowable operating hours based on deterministic reverse math."  
* Reason: The calculation model relies on standard electrical conversion formulas, not predictive machine learning models.  
* Before review: "Client-side predictive algorithms."  
* After review: "Client-side calculation algorithms."  
* Reason: The reverse-budget math is deterministic arithmetic, not prediction. "Predictive" suggests machine learning, which evaluators may expect to see.

### Biggest remaining risk

The primary technical risk is ensuring the usability and accuracy of the multi-appliance allocation algorithm. When a user enters a total budget alongside multiple appliances with varying power draws, prioritizing operating hours across high-draw versus continuous appliances must remain simple and intuitive.

### Three unanswered questions

1. How can the project help users that have shared electricity meters/tenants that split bills?  
2. What level of accuracy counts as "good enough" for the calculator?  
3. Does the project need approval or acknowledgment from ILPI to use its name or branding in the app, or should the app be presented as an independent tool?
