### Selected problem

Electricity consumers in Iligan City experience severe financial risk and administrative friction due to the gap between static utility tools and local operational realities. 

### Selection rationale

We selected the first candidate problem because it directly addresses an immediate, high-friction financial risk that regularly impacts a broad base of Iligan City electricity consumers. With direct access to students, boarders, landlords, and local families, our team can easily gather empirical data on household energy budgeting habits and payment lag issues, whereas hazard reporting and informational mapping rely heavily on municipal department cooperation and city-maintained datasets that are difficult to access within a single semester. Furthermore, analyzing and addressing this problem falls well within our team's analytical and technical readiness, allowing us to evaluate consumer pain points independently without requiring unreleased utility APIs. Ultimately, the other candidate problems were passed over due to high administrative dependencies, whereas electricity bill shocks and disconnection risks represent a pressing, self-contained problem that we can effectively research and solve within the project timeframe.

## 3\. Preliminary Project Proposal

### Working project title

**Iligan WattsApp: Reverse-Budgeting and Payment Risk Mitigation for ILPI Consumers**

### Problem statement

Residential electricity consumers, budget-conscious families, and boarding house tenants in Iligan City face financial risk and operational friction due to limitations in local utility management tools. Currently, users estimate electricity costs using static forward-calculators or physical meter checks, and settle bills via third-party e-wallets. However, forward-calculators fail to calculate reverse budget constraints—such as max daily operating hours for specific appliances within a fixed Peso limit. Furthermore, third-party payment gateways suffer from a 24–48 hour posting lag to ILPI internal ledger, exposing users who pay near due dates to accidental line disconnections, reconnection fees, and mandatory visits to clearing desks in Pala-o or Gaisano Mall. The desired improvement is a client-side mobile application featuring a reverse-budgeting engine and a cryptographically signed receipt verification card to bridge ledger delays. Key assumptions requiring validation include field crews' willingness to accept on-screen cryptographic verification proof and user consistency in entering appliance ratings.

### Proposed solution

A mobile application designed for Iligan City electricity consumers that operates without requiring expensive smart hardware or direct utility API access. The app uses **client-side predictive algorithms**, **local device storage (SQLite/Hive)**, and **cryptographic proof verification** to solve both appliance budget planning and disconnection risks. The app solves **budgeting inaccuracies** by converting a target Peso limit ($PHP$) into exact daily operating hours for specific appliances using reverse math. It eliminates **disconnection risks** from GCash/Maya posting lags by directing urgent payments to instant cash windows in Pala-o or Gaisano, while generating a cryptographically signed ($SHA-256$) receipt card as verifiable on-site proof for field crews while the ledger syncs.

## 5\. Initial Scope

### Intended users and setting

The intended users are residential electricity consumers in the City of Iligan. In particular it would be  budget conscious households, families, and boarding house tenants, especially students who want to manage their electricity consumption and avoid payment related inconveniences like getting one's power cut because of a missed payment. The application is intended to be used on personal mobile devices, allowing users to estimate appliance operating hours based on a fixed electricity budget, monitor payment information, and keep payment receipts accessible even without a direct connection to the Iligan Light and Power, Inc. systems.

### 

### In-scope capabilities

1. **Electricity Reverse Budget Calculator:**  Allows users to enter a target electricity budget in their estimated electricity rate, and appliance wattage to calculate the approximate daily operating hours permitted within their chosen budget.  
2. **Appliance and Consumption Planner:**  Allows users to add appliances, enter their wattage and planned usage, and estimate their electricity consumption and projected costs for better planning.  
3. **Locally Saved Tracker:**  Allows users to save their electricity budgets, appliance details, and previous calculations locally on their devices for future reference without requiring an online account.  
4. **Payment Receipt Organizer and Verification Card:** Allows users to record payment details and generate a receipt card containing the entered transaction information, a timestamp, and a SHA-256 based integrity check to help identify whether the recorded information has been altered.  
5. **Due Date Reminders:** Provides reminders for upcoming payment deadlines and displays guidance about payment options, including official payment channels and in-person payment locations, subject to verification of their current availability and procedures.  
   

### Out-of-scope capabilities

1. **Direct ILPI Account Integration:** The application will not connect directly to ILPI's internal billing system, customer accounts, or payment ledger because authorized API access is not assumed to be available.  
2. **Actual Electricity Bill Retrieval and Payment Processing:** The application will not retrieve official outstanding balances, process payments, or automatically confirm that a payment has been posted to ILPI's records.  
3. **Official Payment Verification and Disconnection Prevention Guarantee:** The generated receipt card will not independently establish that ILPI has received or accepted a payment. It will not guarantee that field personnel will recognize the card or that a disconnection will be prevented.

### 

### Expected deliverables

1. A working mobile application prototype with the aforementioned expected functionality. Noticeably in their prototype form.  
2. A local database for saving appliance records, budget plans, calculations, and user inputted payment details.  
3. A payment receipt organizer and SHA-256 based integrity checking feature.  
4. User interface designs and documentation describing the application's features, calculations, limitations, and intended workflows.  
5. A technical document explaining the reverse budgeting formulas, data storage structure, and receipt integrity-checking process.  
6. Testing documentation covering calculation accuracy, data persistence, usability, and receipt verification behavior.  
7. A final project report presenting the implementation, testing results, user feedback, limitations, and recommendations for future development.

## 6\. Review and Next Steps

### Change made after reviewing the proposal

- Before review: "Eliminates disconnection risks"  
- After review: "Helps reduce late-payment mistakes."  
- Reason: "Eliminates" cannot be supported without ledger access, and the out-of-scope section already disclaims any prevention guarantee.  
    
- Before review: "Client-side predictive algorithms."  
- After review: "Client-side calculation algorithms."  
- Reason: The reverse-budget math is deterministic arithmetic, not prediction. "Predictive" suggests machine learning, which evaluators may expect to see.

### Biggest remaining risk

The biggest risk is whether ILPI would accept the hashed receipts at all. The receipt card is the project's main answer to the posting-lag problem, yet it depends entirely on ILPI staff (billing personnel, and clearing desk officers) treating a self-generated record as meaningful.  
If ILPI does not accept them, the receipt feature is reduced to a personal record-keeping tool, and a core part of the project that directly responds to the disconnection problem, loses its value.

### Three unanswered questions

1. How can the project help users that have shared electricity meters/tenants that split bills?  
2. What level of accuracy counts as "good enough" for the calculator?  
3. Does the project need approval or acknowledgment from ILPI to use its name or branding in the app, or should the app be presented as an independent tool?
